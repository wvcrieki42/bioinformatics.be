// The author

Prof. Dr. ir. Wim Van Criekinge

Wim holds a Master's degree in Chemical Engineering from Ghent University, where he also earned his Ph.D. in Molecular Biology in 1998. In 2000 he founded and heads the laboratory for computational genomics and bioinformatics (BIOBIX) as well as the core sequencing facility (NXTGNT), and is appointed professor in computational genomics and bioinformatics.

His lab was the first to employ routine whole-epigenomics sequencing at scale, applied to age-related diseases such as oncology and rheumatoid arthritis, and to longevity. He is a founder and partner of the Novalis life-science investment funds, translating his expertise into biotech start-ups with successful exits — among them Devgen, MDxHealth, Genohm, Wobblebase, OHMX.Bio and Wintercircus.

This Guide is his living textbook for the bioinformatics course, updated each academic year. He created the Bioinformatics Programming Challenge (BPC) in 2003 — Homo Ludens Bioinformaticus.

// How this book was written

A textbook, co-written with a class and an AI

The Guide started as a collaborative writing project with the class of 2024. After each lecture, a group of students — each with a defined role — drafted a chapter from the slides, primary literature and reference texts, using lecture-capture and generative AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Llama) inside a shared Overleaf workspace. Each draft was presented and critiqued at the start of the next lecture.

After the project, Wim added the introduction, the chapter on genomic codecs, the epilogue and the programming appendices (with Drs. ir. Menno Van Damme), then edited the whole into the book you can read today.

Honest about the AI

The cover was generated by DALL·E — and the book opens by cataloguing its mistakes as the first exercise: impossible base-pairing, and a left-handed (Z-DNA) helix where B-DNA should be. AI is a tool here, used openly and checked, not trusted blindly.
