Bioinformatics Introduction

Where biology meets computation. From the first need to read DNA by hand to machines that sequence billions of bases overnight — and why all of it matters for medicine, food and the planet.

🧪

What is bioinformatics?

The discipline at the intersection of biology, computer science and statistics — turning sequence into knowledge.

📈

A data explosion

Sequencing got exponentially cheaper, outpacing Moore's law and creating a golden age of biology.

🌍

Why it matters

Diagnostics, drug design, crops, conservation — computation now underpins the life sciences.

Three generations of sequencing

How we learned to read the genome — faster, longer, cheaper.

A field of three circles

Bioinformatics lives where biology, computing and statistics overlap.

The cost of a genome

Per-genome cost fell faster than computing got cheaper.

Key takeaways

  • Bioinformatics is the computational discipline that makes sense of biological sequence and structure data.
  • Three sequencing generations — Sanger, NGS, and long-read — each changed what questions we could ask.
  • Falling sequencing costs outpaced Moore's law, triggering today's data-rich "golden age of biology."
  • Its impact spans medicine, agriculture, environmental conservation and fundamental research.
