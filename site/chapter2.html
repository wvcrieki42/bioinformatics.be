02 Chapter Two

Databases

Before you can analyse a sequence, you have to store and find it. From plain-text FASTA records to relational BioSQL schemas and the NoSQL future — how biology's data is organised.

🗂️

Primary databases

GenBank, EMBL, DDBJ for nucleotides; UniProt for proteins — the archives of raw sequence.

🧱

Flat files vs. relational

FASTA and GenBank flat files are simple; relational databases add structure, queries and integrity.

🔮

Toward NoSQL

Key-value, document and column stores handle the scale and flexibility modern genomics demands.

Anatomy of a FASTA record

The simplest, most universal sequence format — a header line and the sequence itself.

A BioSQL-style schema

Relational tables link entries, references and ontology terms.

Phred quality scores

Q = −10·log₁₀(P error). Higher is better; Q30 = 99.9% accurate.

Key takeaways

  • FASTA is the lingua franca: a '>' header line plus the raw sequence.
  • Primary, secondary and specialised databases serve different layers of biological knowledge.
  • Relational schemas like BioSQL bring structure, integrity and cross-references to sequence data.
  • Quality values (Phred/Q-scores) quantify confidence in each sequenced base.
