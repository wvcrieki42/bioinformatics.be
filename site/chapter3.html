03 Chapter Three

Score Matrices

How do you score a match between two residues? Substitution matrices encode which swaps evolution tolerates — the difference between identity and true similarity.

🎯

Identity vs. similarity

Two sequences can be similar without being identical — conservative substitutions still preserve function.

🧮

PAM & BLOSUM

Two families of matrices, derived differently: PAM from evolutionary models, BLOSUM from conserved blocks.

🌒

The Twilight Zone

Below ~20–25% identity, homology becomes hard to distinguish from chance.

A substitution matrix, visualised

Positive scores (cyan) reward likely substitutions; negatives (magenta) penalise unlikely ones. Diagonal = identical residues.

The Twilight Zone

As alignment length grows, the % identity needed to be confident of homology drops — below the curve lies uncertainty.

Key takeaways

  • Substitution matrices score residue pairs by how often evolution swaps them.
  • Positive scores reward conservative changes; the diagonal rewards identity.
  • PAM is built from an evolutionary model; BLOSUM from blocks of conserved alignments.
  • Below the Twilight Zone (~20–25% identity), distinguishing homology from chance gets risky.
