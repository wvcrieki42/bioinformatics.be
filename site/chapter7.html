07 Chapter Seven

Proteins

Sequence is one dimension; function lives in three. How a chain of amino acids folds into a working machine — and why predicting that fold is one of biology's grand challenges.

🔗

Why proteins?

They do the work of the cell — catalysis, structure, signalling, transport.

🌀

How do they fold?

Sequence dictates structure; folding seeks a low-energy, functional shape.

🔬

Protein models

Homology modelling and structure prediction infer 3D shape from sequence and relatives.

Four levels of protein structure

From a linear chain to an assembled complex.

The folding funnel

Many unfolded states funnel down an energy landscape to one native fold.

Hydropathy → membrane spans

Stretches of hydrophobic residues (above the line) predict transmembrane helices.

Key takeaways

  • Proteins are the cell's workhorses; their function depends on 3D shape.
  • Structure is organised in four levels, from primary sequence to quaternary assembly.
  • Folding is a search down an energy funnel toward the native state.
  • Sequence-based methods (hydropathy, homology modelling) predict structure and topology.
