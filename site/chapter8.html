08 Chapter Eight

Genomic Codecs: HMMs & SCFG

Genomes are written in a language. Hidden Markov Models decode genes from hidden states; stochastic grammars capture RNA structure; and even fractal geometry hides in the sequence.

🎰

Hidden states

You see the symbols (bases); the state that emitted them — gene or not — is hidden.

🗣️

Grammars of life

The Chomsky hierarchy frames why RNA folding needs context-free, not just regular, grammars.

❄️

Fractal dimension

Self-similar structure recurs across scales — measurable even in genomic data.

The occasionally dishonest casino

A classic HMM: two hidden states (Fair / Loaded die) with transition probabilities; each emits observable rolls.

The Chomsky hierarchy

RNA secondary structure needs context-free power — nested, not just linear.

Fractal self-similarity

The Sierpiński triangle — a non-integer dimension (~1.585).

Key takeaways

  • HMMs model sequences as hidden states emitting observable symbols — ideal for gene finding.
  • The Viterbi algorithm recovers the most probable hidden-state path (e.g. gene vs. intergenic).
  • RNA structure prediction needs context-free grammars (SCFGs), a step up the Chomsky hierarchy.
  • Fractal geometry offers another lens on the self-similar structure within genomes.
